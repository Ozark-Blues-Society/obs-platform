# Moderating classified listings

Members post listings in the classifieds: musicians wanted, gear, merch, street team and member
connect. Every new listing waits for a moderator, and nobody else sees it until a moderator
approves it. Moderators and administrators review listings on the **Classifieds queue** screen.

Open it from the admin menu, or by adding `/admin/classifieds` to the website's address, for example
`https://<the Society's website>/admin/classifieds`, and sign in if the website asks. Anyone else
who opens it is told that only moderators and administrators can review classified listings. An
administrator gives the Moderator role on the [Roles](roles.md) screen.

## The queue

**Waiting for review** lists the listings no moderator has decided on yet, oldest first. Each one
shows its category, who sent it (first name and last initial) and when, its title and price, its
photo, what the poster wrote, and how people should contact the poster: by email through the
website (their address stays private) or by phone. Read the listing and look at the photo the way a
visitor would: an approved listing appears on the classifieds with this title, text, price and
photo.

The board's rules for the classifieds: every member can post, at any membership level, in all five
categories, and a listing runs for 60 days.

## Approving a listing

Select **Approve**. The listing goes on the classifieds straight away, for 60 days, and the poster
gets an email saying it's live and until when. The screen confirms it, and the listing leaves the
queue.

## Rejecting a listing

1. Select **Reject**.
2. Under **Reason for the poster**, say why, and what would make the listing acceptable. The
   poster gets your reason by email, word for word, so write it to them, for example "Instruments
   for sale go in the Gear category, with a price. Please post it again there." Keep it to 500
   characters.
3. Select **Reject and email the poster**.

The listing stays off the classifieds and leaves the queue. To change your mind before sending,
select **Cancel**. The poster can post the listing again with changes, and it comes back to the
queue.

## After a listing goes live

An approved listing comes down by itself after 60 days. Five days before, the poster gets an email
and can renew it on their **My listings** page, which keeps it up for another 60 days. A renewed
listing doesn't come back to the queue: nothing in it has changed since you approved it. If the
poster changes anything in a listing, it comes back to the queue for you to review again. Posters
can also remove their own listings at any time.

## If something doesn't go through

- **"The poster changed … while you were looking at it"**: the poster edited the listing after
  your screen showed it, so nothing was decided. The queue now shows the new version. Read it again,
  then approve or reject it.
- **"… was already approved" or "… was already rejected"**: someone else decided on it first.
  Nothing more to do.
- **"That listing was deleted by its poster"**: nothing to decide.
- **"The email to the poster didn't go out"**: your decision stands, but the poster wasn't told. An
  administrator can let them know.
- **"Its photo can't be shown here"**: the photo was replaced after the listing was sent, or the
  website couldn't load it. A replaced photo never goes live, so if you approve the listing, it may
  appear without one. If the photo matters, reject the listing with a reason asking the poster to
  post it again with the photo.

## What everyone sees on the classifieds

Approved listings appear on the **Classifieds** page of the website, newest first, with filters
for the five categories. Anyone can read them, members or not. Each listing shows its category,
title, price, details and photo, and the poster's first name and last initial, never their email
address.

Anyone can write to a poster from the listing's page, members or not (decision 0009). They give
their name, their email address and a message, and the website emails it to the poster, who can
simply reply. The poster's own address stays private until they reply. If the poster chose
**phone**, people who are signed in see the phone number on the poster's profile instead.

Because anyone can write, the website keeps spam down without any outside service: each email
address or internet connection can send up to 10 messages a day, and each listing can get up to 20.
The email tells the poster that the sender's address hasn't been checked, and links in messages
appear as plain text, not as links.
